Quantum · Project 03 · Credit risk

Turning a loan application into points.

A credit scorecard is the oldest working model in banking. This one is built end to end on 1,000 real German loan applications, the way a credit risk team builds it, and then I spend most of the effort trying to prove it isn't real.

How it works

01Coarse classing

Every question becomes a few bins. Numeric ones are binned so risk only moves one way: a lender won't sign a scorecard where risk rises, falls, then rises with loan size.

02Weight of Evidence

Each bin gets a score for how well it separates people who repay from people who don't. Checking account: 12% bad with no account, 51% when overdrawn.

03Logistic regression

Picks the characteristics, with a rule: safer evidence must always push the score up. Anything that flips meaning in the fit is thrown out.

04Stability check

The whole pipeline is rebuilt 25 times. A characteristic that survives in under half is dropped; that removed telephone (36%).

05Points

600 points means 50:1 odds of repaying, and every 20 points doubles them. So the 9 characteristics simply add up: scores run from 348 to 655.

06Cutoff by cost

Funding a bad loan costs 5× what turning away a good customer does. That, not accuracy, sets the approval line.

The cutoff is chosen on the training book and simply paid for on the holdout; tuning it on the holdout would flatter the cost (0.503 vs 0.530). The readable scorecard also edges out gradient boosting (AUC 0.780 vs 0.767), because 700 rows is where black boxes have least room to win. 1,000 rows is not a book: every number carries an interval, and there is no reject inference or out-of-time test.
